## 4. B1-ii — PROPOSED RULING ON THE LANGUAGE CLAUSE (three options)

The clause under decision, `AUTHOR-GUIDE.md:6–7`:

> Writing a program against this guide is not a use of an adopted language.

### OPTION α — THE CLAUSE STANDS, WITH ITS REASON MADE EXPLICIT

> What R1 adopted is a repaired **implementation base**, not a **language**. A language at
> this lane's own standard is a published statute — reader grammar, refusal-code vocabulary
> and datatypes, bounds, identifier and keyword identity, result shapes — and the 28-place
> semantic-extractability register is precisely the list of what is not yet written. Until
> that register is discharged, writing a program against this guide is use of an adopted
> *evaluator*, not of an adopted *language*. The guide's sentence is amended only to say so.

*Consequence:* preserves the lane's hardest-won ceiling; makes the adoption's limit legible;
costs one added clause per affected banner. It also fixes the phrase future rounds will
quote — "adopted evaluator, unadopted statute."

### OPTION β — THE CLAUSE IS REPLACED BY A SCOPED TRUTH

> Replace with: *"Programs written against this guide run on the owner-adopted R1 base. The
> language they are written in is not itself adopted: no grammar, refusal-code vocabulary, or
> result shape has been ruled portable, and nothing here licenses a portability or
> independent-implementation claim (R1 adoption Rider 1)."*

*Consequence:* more accurate to a reader who observes that P4 was in fact authored against
this guide and ran on the adopted base. Risk: "runs on the adopted base" is one careless
quotation away from "is an adopted language" — the exact inflation Rider 1 forbids.

### OPTION γ — THE CLAUSE IS STRUCK AND THE QUESTION DEFERRED

> Strike the sentence. Add nothing. The standing of the *language* as against the adopted
> *base* is registered as an open constitutional question and joins the deficit register
> agenda; no banner asserts anything about it either way.

*Consequence:* honest silence, no new law; but it removes a guard the guide currently
carries, and silence in a banner reads as permission to a reader in a hurry. If this option
is taken, the guard should be restated somewhere that a program author cannot miss.

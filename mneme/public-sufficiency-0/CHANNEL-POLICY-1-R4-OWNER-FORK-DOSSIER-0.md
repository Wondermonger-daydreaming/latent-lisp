## D. The retroactivity distinction (the fork's hinge)

**Constitutive retroactivity** — a later policy changing what an earlier
act *was* (creating/removing historical standing; imposing a later receipt
requirement on an earlier event). Presumptively prohibited by R-1's
no-retroactive-motion clause. **Present-day classificatory use** — after
adoption, describing the *current known state* of an old artifact in §1
vocabulary, historical record untouched, uncertainty preserved as
uncertainty. Not "historical correction" unless something was false under
the law then in force — and the eras show there mostly WAS no law then in
force to be false under. The commission's test sentence — *"History keeps
the vocabulary it actually used; the present uses the vocabulary its
current law requires"* — **is licensed by the record** [annex]: every
specimen's historical wording survives quotation intact while its current
state is statable in §1 terms or as explicit uncertainty.

## E–F. The options, tested against all twelve specimens

**OPTION A — strict prospective / vocabulary sealed.** Preserves history
perfectly and goes **silent exactly where an outside reader needs answers**
[annex]: the stale served GRAMMAR, the still-served candidate corpus, the
22 unrepositoried files — under A, *no current-vocabulary report is
possible* for any of them without a separate owner act each time. The
far-side readback cannot be phrased. **Consequence: permanent vocabulary
bifurcation; the readback question becomes unaskable.**

**OPTION B — prospective + non-rewriting savings + evidence-bounded
current classification.** Operative on adoption; nothing historical
rewritten; from commencement, *current* publication-state reports use §1
vocabulary for old and new alike **where surviving evidence supports the
classification**; unknowns stay explicit ("authorization unproved,"
"transport unproved," "historical publication state unresolved"). Yields a
usable, honest sentence for **all twelve specimens** [annex]. **Two costs,
shown before ruling:** (1) the mirror-visible MA0 corpus classifies today
as *transported; publication-authorization unproved* — "transported but
not published" — until/unless a distinct owner act authorizes it (a cheap,
prospective, expressly-separate companion act; see §K note); (2) B
requires one vocabulary addition the candidate lacks: an
**INAPPLICABLE / not-in-channel** state — else the terminal adoption
instrument (bytes nowhere) and the tracked `_staging/` subtree would be
falsely reported as publication *failures* rather than objects outside
the channel's domain.

**OPTION C — full retrospective reconciliation.** **Legally unavailable,
not merely unwise** [annex]: §5.1's "authorized source identity" has **no
Era-A referent** — 98% of publication events would require evidence that
is not merely lost but never existed (working-tree states committed
nowhere); and incident `a14bb55d` is unclassifiable by a state machine C
claims to apply exhaustively. C also collides with R-1 consequence 3.

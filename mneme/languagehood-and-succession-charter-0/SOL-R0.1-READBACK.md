## 4. Readback findings

### SOL-R01-01 — RATIFICATION BLOCKER — Authority and claim-class grammar still conflict

**Sites:** Charter §F.1 items 1–2; `CLAIM-CEILING-R0.1.md` languagehood rows; Owner Docket F-8; Succession Docket §3.

The charter classifies languagehood and jurisdiction as **PROPOSED HOLDING**, and F-3a/F-3b exist precisely because neither is yet ratified. Section F.1 nevertheless labels the qualified languagehood and jurisdiction sentences **CC-2 — licensed now**, then calls their fully annotated forms CC-3. The compact ceiling likewise puts candidate P1 and proposed W-04 language in the column titled “Strongest licensed wording.” A proposition cannot be both unlicensed pending ratification and presently licensed merely by shortening it.

F-8 contains the same category slip in miniature: P1's candidate design-provenance sentence is called “licensed by a candidate document.” Candidate text can propose a ceiling; it cannot license one with constitutional force.

The sharpest surviving SOL-HR-01 instance is in `SUCCESSION-DOCKET-R0.1.md` §3:

> “Open-ended” is forbidden as a result description at every strength, including for the composite (Charter-P1 …; Ruling 8).

Ruling 8 supplies the finite-run refusal **without the composite qualification**. The stronger every-strength/composite ban belongs only to P1 candidate text. The main charter C.21 gets this distinction right; the Succession Docket launders it back into present law.

**Smallest repair:** make languagehood/jurisdiction CC-3 until owner ratification; put “no presently licensed constitutional compression; proposed wording W-04/W-02” in the ceiling; describe F-8's sentence as candidate-proposed design wording; mark the composite-strength ban `[PROPOSED]` and cite Ruling 8 only for its narrower finite-run rule.

### SOL-R01-02 — RATIFICATION BLOCKER — W-01 is not mechanically closed

**Sites:** Charter §A.1, §B.3, rungs 6–10; `CLAIM-CEILING-R0.1.md` OPEN rows.

Section A.1 promises exactly six primary tokens and says the two OPEN sub-annotations are **OPEN-UNOPENED** and **OPEN-JURISDICTION-CLOSED**, “never dropped.” The document then uses `REFUSED` rather than `REFUSED CLAIM` for all four B.3 inversions, and repeatedly writes bare `UNOPENED` / `JURISDICTION-CLOSED` instead of the declared forms.

**Smallest repair:** token sweep only. Replace B.3 `REFUSED` labels with `REFUSED CLAIM`; normalize every OPEN sub-annotation to the exact declared form; add a documentary check that rejects non-legend primary/status tokens and truncated OPEN sub-annotations.

**Sol's recommendation, relayed and adopted by the owner:**

> The correct architectural move
>
> We should stop trying to infer normative meaning from arbitrary rendered
> GFM and instead define a small permitted source language for governed
> documents.
>
> The beautiful irony is that Lisp+ already taught us the principle:
>
> The renderer displays the charter; it is not itself the law.
>
> A bounded governance-markup profile could simply require:
>
> UTF-8 in a declared normalization form;
> no Unicode format characters (Cf) or bidi controls;
> no raw HTML;
> no character entities inside reserved words such as Status;
> only a specified subset of Markdown constructs;
> mechanically canonical tables and Status declarations;
> immediate refusal of anything outside that subset.
>
> Then the checker does not need to understand whether `<template hidden>`
> is visible under browser semantics. It says: "That construct is not in
> the document language." Finito. The goblin is denied admission at the
> gate instead of receiving a forty-page visibility hearing.
>
> My recommended disposition would therefore be:
>
> Do not continue the R0.18 arms race as currently framed. Replace its
> open-ended rendering model with one final restricted-input-profile
> repair, verify the clean five-file corpus and inherited controls, then
> open the owner docket.
>
> After that, return attention to the actual language: independent
> implementation and independent inhabitation. The audit did yield one
> excellent lesson—governance documents also require specification-owned
> semantic jurisdiction—but the lesson is now earned. We do not need to
> discover whether a Status declaration can be concealed inside an SVG
> `<foreignObject>` while Mercury is retrograde.

**Triage (Sol §8).** Classified *"MUST DISPOSE BEFORE CHARTER RATIFICATION"*,

**Question.** When taken up, the question is whether the §A legend is adopted
for **constitutional use** — i.e. whether its six-token classification, applied

citable as such outside this charter — or whether it remains a candidate
finding aid whose classifications bind nothing beyond this document.

**Options.**

- **Option A — Adopt the legend's constitutional use**, carrying §A.2's
  disclaimers inside the adoption (it records where standing was conferred and
  by whom; it confers none), with a standing rule that the legend is
  **re-derived, not hand-edited**, after every owner ruling, and a header
  stating its generation source and date.
- **Option B — No adopted cross-lane use**; the legend stays this document's
  internal finding aid, and standing is read per-lane from each lane's own
  instruments.

**Evidence available.** `AUTHORITY-INDEX.md` ("Nothing in this index confers
standing"), `MANY-ACTS-0-STANDING.md` ("A banner cannot answer the question"),

**Recommendation.** **A**, on the re-derivation rule — the legend's danger is
staleness, and staleness is curable by generation, not by care.

## F-4 — Succession vocabulary, and the permanence doctrine

**Triage (Sol §8 + §9.3).** F-4 was classified *"MUST DISPOSE BEFORE CHARTER
RATIFICATION"*, disposition: *"Genuine vocabulary policy once Article 13 is
correctly demoted from candidate 'law.'"* The demotion is performed in R0.1 (§C.22, §E rung 9:
Charter-P1 Art. 13 is candidate text, `[PROPOSED]`, not present law). Sol's
missing-fork §9.3 is folded in here as directed: *"Once P1 Article 13 is
correctly demoted, the owner must decide whether its 'no experiment can ever
grant succession' rule is adopted constitutional policy or merely the present
charter's recommendation. This may be folded into revised F-4."*

**Question.** Two halves, answered together because the second changes what the
first means. When taken up, the question is: **(i) permanence** — does Art.
13's rule that succession is not a node and that *no experiment can ever grant
it* become adopted constitutional policy, or does it remain this charter's
`[PROPOSED]` recommendation; and **(ii) vocabulary** — may "successor" appear
as *marked research-trajectory* language ("a trajectory toward a Lisp
successor"), or is the word fully reserved to Article-13-style refusals?

**Options.**

- **Option A — Adopt permanence; permit marked-trajectory use.** The title can
  never be conferred by an experiment, and precisely because it can never be an
  empirical result, the word is safe to use inside an explicit intent frame
  ("trajectory toward", "aspires to") — never in a result sentence, never in
  the title of an evidence-bearing document.
- **Option B — Adopt permanence; fully reserve the word.** The strictest
  combination: a permanent constitutional refusal, and the word appears only in
  refusals.
- **Option C — Leave permanence `[PROPOSED]`; permit marked-trajectory use.**
  The project does not constitutionalize a "never" on the authority of an
  unadopted predecessor charter; the word is usable only inside the mandatory
  frame, and the title remains unearned in fact at every rung.
- **Option D — Leave permanence `[PROPOSED]`; fully reserve the word.**
  Nothing adopted, nothing said.

**Evidence available.** Art. 13's rationale (a title conferred by a tradition
over time — sustained lineage, use, extensibility, influence: §C.22); the
demotion at §C.22 and §E rung 9 (*"R0's 'permanently' is withdrawn as present
law"*), which is what makes the permanence half a genuine question rather than
an assumed one; W-09's own referral of the question to owner policy; the
commission's §5 phrasing (whether "successor" is reserved exclusively for an
achieved rung or may also name a clearly marked research trajectory); rung 9's
three-way collision showing how the word behaves when compressed; SOL-HR-01 on
laundering candidate authority into present law.

**Consequences.** A: honest ambition stays sayable and the title is placed
permanently outside evidence's reach, which removes the incentive to ever
design an experiment for it; the marked frame becomes one more qualifier
rider-7 discipline must keep attached. B: zero drift risk; the project's
aspirational documents must paraphrase around their actual aspiration, which
breeds euphemism. C: the vocabulary is usable now and the constitutional
question stays open for a later record; the cost is that "never" remains
`[PROPOSED]` and must be re-marked at every occurrence. D: nothing changes;
maximal conservatism, maximal euphemism pressure.

**Recommendation.** **C.** The vocabulary half is R0's recommendation carried
unchanged (permit only the greppable marked frame — the exact phrase
"trajectory toward" or "aspires to" within the same sentence). On the newly
folded permanence half the drafter's recommendation diverges toward
non-adoption: a permanent constitutional "never" adopted on the strength of an
unadopted predecessor's article is the precise move SOL-HR-01 exists to catch,
and the practical protection the project needs — that no directly
authenticated source named by the charter licenses any succession sentence
(W-14 form, bounded at R0.3) — is already supplied by rung 9's standing
REFUSED CLAIM entry without any permanence doctrine at all.

**Default if deferred.** **D.** The word stays fully reserved; Art. 13 travels
as candidate text, `[PROPOSED]`, binding nothing; rung 9 continues to hold the
compression out.

## F-6 (deferred, reframed) — What is earned by same-substrate and cross-runtime independence?

**Reframing (Sol §8 + §9.2, folding the F-12 missing fork; charter §D.0a).**
R0 asked one question — does an independently seeded second codebase on the
same Common Lisp substrate earn a named intermediate standing? Sol requires the
question be **separated into profiles**, because they are earned by different
work and licence different sentences:

1. **Independent-codebase profile** — a second, independently coded judge in
   the same implementation language on the same runtime.
2. **Implementation-language profile** — a judge written in a different
   implementation language.
3. **Runtime-implementation profile** — the *same* implementation run on a
   different CL runtime (e.g. CCL rather than SBCL): this tests **runtime
   portability** and is recorded as a subordinate annotation on the
   implementation axis, never as substrate-axis credit and never as "same
   substrate, nothing" (§D.0a).
4. **Substrate profile** — a different-substrate implementation, which is what
   §C.15 substrate independence actually names.

**Recorded options (not put).** Named intermediate standings per profile, each
with its full sentence fixed in the ruling that grants it and none compressible
to "second implementation" (the LCI/0 pattern: *"independently seeded under
shared normative infrastructure, same substrate"*) — versus no intermediate
standing at any profile, same-substrate work recorded as candidate preparation
only.

**Evidence available.** LCI/0 as the existing precedent (independently seeded,
same substrate, closure owner-authorized and internally audited, external pass
waived); PJ0's adopted gate, which expects an independently-seeded CL
implementation against the full vector set; Ruling 3's boundary (the AOI does
not constitute a second implementation of the full judge); §D.0a; W-08's
correction of the R-P5 independence row, which is the finding that forced the
profile split.

**Where it resolves.** In the authorizing ruling of the campaign that first
produces work at one of these profiles — no present claim depends on naming the
intermediate rung.

**Until then.** No intermediate standing exists; §D's matrix records
each axis as it stands, and no profile name may be used in a result sentence.
